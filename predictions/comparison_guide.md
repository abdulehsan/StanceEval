## 1. Column Dictionary

| Column Name | Source Pipeline / Model | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `row_index` | Dataset | The 0-based index of the tweet in the 352-row blind test set (`ground_truth.csv`). |
| `original_tweet` | Dataset | The raw Arabic tweet text from the test set. |
| `allam_interpretation` | **ALLaM Interpreter Pipeline** | Local Arabic interpretation and cultural context resolution generated by `allam-7b-instruct-preview`. |
| `gemma_stage1_translation` | **Two-Stage Same-Model Pipeline** | English literal translation and mental rewrite generated by `gemma-4-31b` via Cerebras (Stage 1). |
| `gemma_debate_claim_argument` | **Debate Pipeline** (Stage 1) | The 1-2 sentence pro/con claim justification written by `gemma-4-31b` via Cerebras. |
| `qwen_debate_challenger_argument` | **Debate Pipeline** (Stage 2) | Critical challenge and alternative readings proposed by `qwen-3-32b` (via Groq) to debate Gemma's claim. |
| `explain_then_classify_stance` | **Two-Stage Same-Model Pipeline** | Final stance classification from `gemma-4-31b` using original tweet + translation. |
| `debate_final_stance` | **Debate Pipeline** (Stage 3) | Consensus stance decided by `gemma-4-31b` acting as final judge on all debate arguments. |
| `baseline_stance` | **Zero-Shot Baseline** (`best_86_61` / `ci_v3`) | The stance predicted by our champion zero-shot classifier prompt in a single call. |

## 2. Pipeline Architectures & System Prompts

### 🛰️ Pipeline 1: Zero-Shot Baseline (`best_86_61` / `ci_v3`)
*   **Architecture**: Single-call, zero-shot classification. Gemma is instructed to mentally rewrite the tweet and classify stance in a single output word.
*   **Parameters**: `gemma-4-31b` via Cerebras, `temperature=0.1`, `max_tokens=10`.
*   **System Prompt**:
```
You are an expert annotator for Arabic stance detection.

### Background Context
The target concerns the 2017–2018 Saudi policy change allowing women to drive. Before June 2018, women were prohibited from driving in Saudi Arabia. Tweets from this period often discuss the royal decree, implementation, licensing, religion, tradition, safety, gender roles, media coverage, and public reaction. They frequently use Saudi dialect, sarcasm, humor, rhetorical questions, and indirect expressions.

### Task
Given an Arabic tweet and a target topic, classify the writer's stance toward the target as exactly one of:

- Favor
- Against
- None

Before assigning a stance, mentally rewrite the tweet into its intended literal meaning while preserving the writer's opinion, sarcasm, dialect, rhetorical intent, and emojis.

Then determine the stance toward the target itself, not toward other people, quoted opinions, related entities, or hashtags.

Guidelines:

- Favor: supports, defends, promotes, or welcomes the target.
- Against: opposes, criticizes, rejects, or mocks the target.
- None: no clear stance toward the target.

Important:

- Determine where praise or criticism is directed. Negative language toward opponents of the target is usually Favor, not Against.
- Hashtags may be ironic or hijacked. Never infer stance from hashtags alone.
- Rhetorical questions, sarcasm, and emojis often convey the writer's true stance. Interpret the intended meaning rather than the literal wording.
- Distinguish reporting from endorsement. Mentioning an event or policy does not by itself express a stance, unless it is framed positively (e.g. promoting, celebrating, or inviting participation), in which case it leans Favor.
- If the stance toward the target cannot reasonably be inferred, output None.

Respond with ONLY one word:

Favor
Against
None

Do not provide any explanation, punctuation, or additional text.
```

### 🛰️ Pipeline 2: Two-Stage Same-Model Pipeline (`explain_then_classify_v1`)
*   **Architecture**: Decomposes reasoning by calling the same model twice. Stage 1 generates a clear English interpretation of dialect and sarcasm. Stage 2 consumes both the original Arabic tweet and the English interpretation to output a single stance label.
*   **Stage 1 parameters**: `gemma-4-31b` via Cerebras, `temperature=0.1`, `max_tokens=384`.
*   **Stage 2 parameters**: `gemma-4-31b` via Cerebras, `temperature=0.1`, `max_tokens=10`.

#### Stage 1 System Prompt (Explanation)
```
You are an expert Arabic language analyst.

### Background Context
The target concerns the 2017–2018 Saudi policy change allowing women to drive. Before June 2018, women were prohibited from driving in Saudi Arabia. Tweets from this period often discuss the royal decree, implementation, licensing, religion, tradition, safety, gender roles, media coverage, and public reaction. They frequently use Saudi dialect, sarcasm, humor, rhetorical questions, and indirect expressions.

### Task
Given an Arabic tweet, rewrite its intended literal meaning in clear English, preserving the writer's opinion, sarcasm, dialect, rhetorical intent, and emojis. Do not classify stance. Do not mention Favor, Against, or None. Only explain what the tweet is actually saying and who or what it is directed at.

Respond with plain explanation only.
```

### 🛰️ Pipeline 3: Debate Pipeline (Gemma-Qwen-Gemma)
*   **Architecture**: A multi-model debate pipeline.
    *   *Stage 1*: Gemma classifies stance and writes a justification (claim argument).
    *   *Stage 2*: Qwen acts as a critical challenger, identifying weaknesses or arguing for the opposite stance.
    *   *Stage 3*: Gemma acts as a final judge, reviewing the original tweet and both arguments to decide.
*   **Stage 1 & 3 Parameters**: `gemma-4-31b` via Cerebras, `temperature=0.1`.
*   **Stage 2 Parameters**: `qwen-3-32b` via Groq, `temperature=0.7`.

#### Stage 1 System Prompt (Claim Generation)
```
You are an expert annotator for Arabic stance detection.

### Background Context
The target concerns the 2017–2018 Saudi policy change allowing women to drive. Before June 2018, women were prohibited from driving in Saudi Arabia. - Favor
- Against
- None

Before assigning a stance, mentally rewrite the tweet into its intended literal meaning while preserving the writer's opinion, sarcasm, dialect, rhetorical intent, and emojis.

Then determine the stance toward the target itself, not toward other people, quoted opinions, related entities, or hashtags.

Guidelines:

- Against: opposes, criticizes, rejects, or mocks the target.

Important:

Respond in exactly this format:

```

#### Stage 2 System Prompt (Challenge Generation)
```
You are a critical reviewer of Arabic stance detection judgments.

You will be given a tweet, a target topic, and another annotator's classification with
their justification. Your job is to challenge it:

- If you believe the classification is wrong, argue for what you believe the correct
  stance is (Favor, Against, or None) and explain why, citing specific words, tone,
  sarcasm, or context in the tweet.
- If you believe the classification is correct, still identify any weaknesses or
  alternative readings in the original justification — do not simply agree without
  scrutiny.

Be concise
```

### 🛰️ Pipeline 4: ALLaM Interpreter Pipeline
*   **Architecture**: Uses a locally hosted `allam-7b-instruct-preview` model as a dialect and cultural interpreter (with Knowledge Base references for entity mapping) to generate an Arabic rewrite of the tweet's hidden intent. This interpretation was then sent to Gemma-4-31b for final stance classification.
*   **ALLaM Parameters**: `allam-7b-instruct-preview` via llama.cpp locally (T=0.1).

#### ALLaM Interpreter System Prompt Template
```
You are an expert in Saudi Arabic dialect, sarcasm, and cultural context.

Given a tweet, explain what the writer literally means beneath the surface — resolve any
sarcasm, dialect-specific phrasing, or rhetorical devices. Preserve the writer's emotional
tone and attitude (e.g. celebratory, mocking, frustrated, joking, worried) — do not flatten
it into a neutral factual summary. Focus on tone and intent, not on classifying any stance.
Do not mention "Favor," "Against," or "None," and do not use evaluative words that imply
approval or disapproval (e.g. "positive," "negative," "disapproving," "supportive") —
describe the tone itself, not a judgment of it.

{kb_reference}
Use any reference information above only to correctly understand what a hashtag or named
entity refers to — it does not tell you how the writer feels about it. The writer may be
using that hashtag or entity ironically, defiantly, or dismissively; your job is still to
read that from the tweet's own wording and tone.
```
